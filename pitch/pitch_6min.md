# KAIO: 6-minute live pitch (draft to rehearse together)

About 780 spoken words at about 130 words per minute. **A** and **B** are a suggested split between the two
speakers. Every number comes from `results/summary.json` (20 real days, mean ± 95 % CI).

| Time | Screen | Speaker and words |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:40** Hook | Dashboard top: 3D Earth, satellite moving, the pass and eclipse pills changing | **A:** "This is the real orbit of Φsat-2, ESA's AI satellite. Every 94 minutes it falls into 35 minutes of darkness. It sees a ground station for about 79 minutes a day. Its cameras produce far more data than it can ever send down. So, for every single image, the satellite has to decide: process it onboard now, keep it, send it, or hand it to a neighbour. That is Problem 1. KAIO is our answer." |
| **0:40–1:35** Real data | "Clouds fool raw entropy" section | **A:** "We didn't use made-up images. We flew the real orbit over 20 days of real NASA imagery: VIIRS, MODIS clouds and FIRMS fire detections. The first thing the real data did was break our first idea. The textbook trick is to keep the most 'information-rich' images, the ones with the highest entropy. But clouds are the most textured scenes, and they cover 64 % of what the satellite sees. Ranked by raw entropy, the top 30 % of images hold less value than a random pick. Multiplying by a land mask that's already onboard fixes it: 79 % of the value, against 82 % for a perfect oracle." |
| **1:35–2:40** How it decides | "How KAIO decides" four steps | **B:** "Every 10 seconds, the E-LOTUS engine does four things. One: it estimates each image's value from two numbers the satellite already has, texture and land fraction. Two: it scores every action by how much it worsens three debts, storage backlog, battery below what the next eclipse needs, and heat above 45 °C, against the value it delivers. That's the drift-plus-penalty rule from Lyapunov optimisation, with one tuning knob, V. Three: an independent safety guard can veto any job, like fault protection on real flight software. Four: every decision is logged with a one-line reason an operator can read." |
| **2:40–3:40** Live demo | Dashboard replay at 2×; then the Raspberry Pi terminal running `live_demo.py` | **A:** "Here's one real day. Blue is KAIO, green is a greedy processor, pink a tuned rule engine. Watch the greedy battery fall to the shutdown line while KAIO stays near 70 %. And this…" *(point at the Pi)* "…is a 50-dollar Raspberry Pi running the actual KAIO flight code right now. Each line is a decision, its reason, and how long the Pi took: about [X] milliseconds. The satellite needs one every 10 seconds." |
| **3:40–4:50** Results, honest | Benchmark KPIs, then the stress grid | **B:** "Over 20 real days, KAIO delivers 85 % of the value the ground passes physically allow. A rule engine with exactly the same information gets 81.5 %. KAIO wins on all 20 days, with no load-shedding and no throttling. Under stress, with weaker solar panels, half the downlink or three times more fires, it still wins every day. With a smaller battery the rule engine collapses, because its thresholds don't retune themselves. And here's where we lose: with a slower GPU or a degraded radiator, the rule engine scores higher, but only by running above the thermal limit for 37 to 81 % of the day. We chose to respect the limit." |
| **4:50–5:35** Real life | README "Real-life deployment" or the four-step panel | **A:** "Could this fly? Every input already exists on a satellite: battery and temperature sensors, a pass schedule uploaded from the ground, a land mask from the GPS position, and an image thumbnail. No GPU and no training, just a few hundred lines that run on a Pi. Next steps: hardware-in-the-loop on a flight-like computer, a learned cloud mask to replace the static land prior, and coordination across a constellation." |
| **5:35–6:00** Close | Dashboard hero | **B:** "Satellites will keep producing more data than they can send. The question is which bits deserve the downlink. KAIO decides, explains why, and never breaks the spacecraft to do it. Thank you." |

## Rehearsal notes
- Fill in **[X] ms** after running `python3 onboard/pi_benchmark.py` on the Pi.
- If you run over time, shorten the "How it decides" segment first. Keep the honest-losses sentence: judges remember it.
- Open the dashboard before going on stage and scroll to each section once. It works offline.
- Start the Pi demo before the pitch with `python3 onboard/live_demo.py --speed 60` so lines are already scrolling.
